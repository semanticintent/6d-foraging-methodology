## The Origin (L0)

**Event:** Recurring parts inventory management issues

**Date:** 2025

**Dimension:** Operational

**Visible Cost:** $119,000

**Context:**

An aviation maintenance facility experienced stockouts, inventory discrepancies, and reliance on manual workarounds. The visible costs were expedited shipping charges and inefficiency overhead.

What appeared to be a contained operational problem cascaded through quality, employee morale, customer relationships, revenue, and regulatory compliance.

## Key Insights

1. **Operational issues rarely stay operational:** What began as inventory management cascaded to quality (aircraft downtime), then customer relationships (airlines reconsidering contracts), then revenue (lost business).

2. **Employee dimension often overlooked:** Technician frustration and overtime spikes were early warning signals that predicted later turnover risk and knowledge loss.

3. **Regulatory exposure compounds:** Minor FAA documentation concerns emerged as a secondary effect of the quality issues, adding compliance costs and scrutiny.

4. **The multiplier was 18.5×:** For every dollar visible in expedited shipping and inefficiency, nearly $19 in total business impact occurred across the cascade.

---

## Patterns Observed

- **The Operational Ripple Pattern:** Operational issue → Quality impact → Customer complaints → Revenue pressure → Cost cutting → Worse operations
- **The Compliance Cascade Pattern:** Regulatory finding → Remediation investment → Resource diversion → Delivery delays → Customer impact

## Outcome

The facility implemented:
- Automated inventory management system
- Preventive maintenance on inventory systems
- Cross-training program to reduce technician frustration
- Customer communication protocols during downtime

Early indicators suggest the cascade is stabilizing, but the total cost of $2.2M significantly exceeded initial expectations of $119K in "operational fixes."
